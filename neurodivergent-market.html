What Sensory-Friendly Actually Means

5 changes that make your business accessible (and profitable)

01

Predictable Environment

Clear signage, consistent layout, no unexpected stimuli. Neurodivergent customers need to know what to expect.

Impact: Reduces anxiety, increases visit frequency
02

Sensory Control

Dim lights, lower music volume, minimal scents. Create "quiet hours" or permanent quiet zones.

Impact: 50% of ALL shoppers prefer quieter stores
03

Communication Options

Written menus, visual schedules, text-based ordering. Not everyone processes verbal info well under sensory load.

Impact: Serves deaf/hard-of-hearing community too
04

Flexible Pacing

No rush, clear time estimates, option to pause. Processing time varies—accommodate it.

Impact: Reduces stress, improves customer satisfaction
05

Staff Training

Educate team on neurodiversity, sensory needs, communication styles. Empathy scales.

Impact: Better service for ALL customers

Implementation Checklist

Start here: Quick wins for your business

✓ Audit Your Environment

Walk through your space. Note: lighting brightness, music volume, scent intensity, visual clutter, unpredictable sounds.

✓ Create Quiet Hours

Test 2-hour windows with dimmed lights, no music, minimal announcements. Walmart does 8-10am—pick what works for you.

✓ Update Signage

Clear, simple, visual. Show sensory-friendly hours prominently. Use symbols + text.

✓ Train Staff

30-minute session on neurodiversity basics. Focus: patience, clear communication, no judgment.

✓ Add Communication Options

Written menus, point-to-order, text-based inquiry. Reduces verbal processing load.

✓ Market It

Only 48% know sensory-friendly options exist. Promote your accommodations. Reach autism/ADHD organizations, parent groups.

✓ Gather Feedback

Anonymous surveys from neurodivergent customers. Iterate based on actual needs.

✓ Measure Results

Track: new customer acquisition during quiet hours, customer satisfaction scores, repeat visit rates.
